## A. Bugs — all fixed

Each was reproduced against a real project during review. A1 was fixed earlier;
A2–A6 are fixed together, and each entry keeps its original description with a
**Fixed** note saying what was done. Nothing in this section is left.

### A1. "Sometimes" and "only at setup" do nothing different

`src/layout/score.ts:101` is the only access rule:

```ts
if (!t.top && r?.access === "often") access++;
```

Only `often` scores. `sometimes` and `setup` are indistinguishable, so a
three-way control offers two behaviours. A user who changes `often` to
`sometimes` sees nothing happen and cannot tell whether the setting works.

**Fix:** make `setup` actively prefer sitting _under_ other trays — a
setup-only tray is the right thing to bury. Then the three values mean keep
clear / neutral / prefer underneath. Update the hint to describe all three and
to say it is a preference the optimizer weighs, not a rule.

### A2. A stale layout gives no hint that the plan needs re-running (fixed)

A layout stored under an older `geometryVersion` keeps its old geometry. The
only notice says the trays "won't mate with ones printed from it: reprint the
whole insert" — it never says _re-run the plan_, which is the actual fix. Two
separate investigations this session (a lip inset, then wall thickness) were
spent on layouts that were simply stale.

**Fix:** say the plan is out of date and offer to re-run it. The layout button
should also read "Update plan" rather than "Keep searching" in that state,
since re-fitting is what is needed.

**Fixed:** the notice now says the plan is out of date, names both geometry
versions and says "Update plan" rebuilds the trays with the current geometry. An
older geometry line counts as needing an update for the button label too.

### A3. The project menu will not close (fixed)

`src/ui/ProjectMenu.tsx` has no outside-click or on-select handler, so the menu
stays open until its own button is clicked again.

**Fix:** close on an outside click and on choosing a project.

**Fixed:** it also closes on Escape, with focus returning to its button.

### A4. The preview banner is unactionable and cannot be dismissed (fixed)

`src/app/App.tsx:318`: "Preview: saved projects and shared links may break
between versions." A user cannot act on it and cannot dismiss it.

**Fix:** remove it, or make it dismissible and say what the user should do
(keep a `.json` export). Leaning towards removing it.

**Fixed:** removed, with its style rule.

### A5. Warnings make the page scroll (fixed)

Several warnings at once push the layout out of view.

**Fix:** collapse or summarise them so the results stay visible.

**Fixed:** the layout messages show the first 3 (every error always, then the
most severe), and the rest fold behind a "Show all N messages" button.

### A6. "Doesn't fit" is reported as fact when it means "not found yet" (fixed)

Measured on a real project (Star Realms, 101 × 195 × 37 mm, stored layout at
geometry 0.4.0): warm-starting from the stored layout is feasible with 9 trays,
yet a cold run fails at 50 000 and at 300 000 iterations. The stored layout was
reached through 4 000 000 cumulative iterations. The box is extremely tight: two
stacks must each total exactly the 35 mm top plane, so only one partition of the
parts works. The old notice said the components "don't fit" and the box was "too
small by N mm", which a user would take as a proven fact and abandon a project
that does fit.

**Fixed:** the message now says no fit was found yet, names the iterations the
run made, says that is not proof, calls the figures the closest attempt so far and
says pressing "Update plan" again searches longer. The search itself is unchanged.
Because a failed run stores nothing, a plain repeat would redo the identical
search, so a user-pressed repeat for the same inputs now runs 3 times the previous
budget (no automatic retry). **Left open:** a box this tight may still not be found
cold within the time cap; finding it is a search problem, not a wording one.

**Search fixed (2026-10-16):** a cold run now solves the Star Realms project at the default
budget on every seed measured (0 of 10 before, 10 of 10 now, in about 170 ms). Investigation:

- _How often it bit._ Not one pathological project. On 20 constructed boxes, each at the
  smallest height that still admits a partition of the trays into 2 or 3 stacks (verified by
  building that tree), the default budget solved 40 of 200 runs (10 seeds each): 4 of the 20
  boxes always, 16 never. Those are the harshest case, with no slack at all; a box one layer
  taller is much easier.
- _Where it failed._ The right partition was reachable but not by one step. The annealer's
  moves change whole stacks (merge, split, swap leaves); no move takes one tray from one stack
  to another, so trading heights between stacks went through splitting a stack off (a whole
  extra stack on the floor: an overflow of a hundred mm that is never accepted) and merging it
  back. In the Star Realms runs the best attempt was one tray (7.87 mm) in the wrong stack, 4.87 mm
  over. On the three-stack boxes a second trap: the start was a 6-stack layout two columns wide,
  an x overflow of 24.7 mm, which only a merge down to three stacks with every stack already
  fitting escapes, while a plain longest-first deal over three stacks overflows by 2.9 mm.
  More iterations do not help (400 000 gave the same counts as 150 000).
- _Fix._ Two moves, `moveItem` and `swapItems` (weight 4 each, of a total of 108), and partition starts
  (`partitionTrees`), tried only when no greedy start is feasible. Each alone is not enough:
  moves alone 162 of 200 and Star Realms 10 of 10; starts alone 140 of 200 and Star Realms 0
  of 10; together 199 of 200 and Star Realms 10 of 10. The regression tests are two tight
  fixtures (`tight-star-realms`, `tight-minis`) solved cold on seeds 1 to 5.
- _Cost._ Fixtures over 100 seeds each, before and after: printed parts 8.73 / 8.89
  (`cards-heavy`), 5.71 / 5.66 (`bits-heavy`), 5.12 / 5.19 (`tiles-minis`), 10.91 / 10.81
  (`small-tiles`); fillers within 0.11; filament within 1.5 %; run time within 3 %
  (252 / 255, 225 / 232, 115 / 115, 235 / 240 ms). Share links of the seed 1 layouts:
  1 787 / 1 795, 1 906 / 1 771, 1 938 / 1 961, 1 961 / 1 969 characters: `small-tiles` and
  `tiles-minis` stay 31 and 39 under 2 000, and what lands where moves with any change to the
  search. `DEFAULT_ITERATIONS` is unchanged.
- _Not done._ One of the 200 constructed runs (the `mini3-2` box, seed 3) still fails at the
  default budget: a box with no slack at all among seven or eight parts can still need a retry.

## B. Design defects — specified in the companion document

Larger than bugs, smaller than features: each produces output a user reported as
wrong. All three are covered by `2026-10-13-minimal-trays-and-growth.md`.

- **B1. Stacked trays get stretched** (done, geometry 0.5.0), so identical
  components came out with different wall thicknesses, and a deck holder grew a
  pocket belonging to no component. Cause: a stack level held exactly one tray.
  Spec §3, §4. **Done:** a level is now a tray at the smallest size that holds
  its contents plus fillers for the rest of its room, and the minis tray from
  the real project comes out 110 × 28.4 with 1.2 mm walls on top of the Bits tray, as
  it does alone. **Also done** (geometry 0.5.1, schema 0.1.8, spec §4.2): the in-tray spare
  compartment is removed (a stretched deck's slack is a spacer, never a pocket), and
  `wallGrowth` is now `wallAbsorb`. The share links needed lossless compaction to stay under
  2 000 characters, and a stretched tray's spacer printed solid, heavier than the pocket
  (`small-tiles` about 100 g more). **Fixed in geometry 0.5.2** (spec §4.3): a spacer is an open
  box under the default `leftover` style, which took about 70 g back; the rest is the stretch
  itself, which the growth policy (C2, done) bounds.
- **B2. Fillers fragment** (**done**, schema 0.1.9, geometry stays 0.5.2, spec §6.2). A real layout
  produced four where two would do: two edge strips that are the same strip in different tree
  leaves, and an interior gap that should have been pushed to the edge. **Done:** connected fillers
  print as one part up to what the bed holds (`mergeFillers`, on by default; `mergeRisers`, off,
  also folds the risers in as an L in elevation), derived at print time and in neither the inputs
  hash nor the stored layout or share link; filler count is a score term of its own (20 000, on top
  of a part's 20 000). The Star Realms project goes from six filler parts after its warm start (four
  in its stored layout, two more in stack levels) to two; `cards-heavy` from 3.5 to 1.7 on average,
  `small-tiles` from 3.3 to 1.2. **Dropped:** rewarding fillers against the box wall; measured over
  40 seeds, it raised the count on `small-tiles` (1.15 to 1.40) while helping `cards-heavy` by 0.13.
  Spec §6.2.
- **B3. One component's trays scatter** (done), differently oriented and in different
  stacks. Same-orientation is a free tiebreak; same-stack is a score trade
  against `access`. Spec §6, §6.1. **Done:** two weak score terms; the three Starter-deck
  trays of the Star Realms project now share an orientation and, as the spec predicted,
  still stand in two stacks, because grouping them would bury two "often" trays.

- **B4. A latent CutFitError at a larger outer** (**done**, geometry 0.5.2, spec §4.3). Four causes
  fixed. The last, greedy channel placement stranding a pocket, is fixed by backing off in
  `placeCutouts`: while an owner has no cut, give up one cut of an owner that has another and place
  again. The property at 1 000 runs passes on all of seeds 1 to 12 (10 of 12 before); inputs are
  cases 4 and 5 of `cutfit.regression.test.ts`. A placement that already cut every owner is
  unchanged, so no version bump and no fixture changes. Not tried: ordering by constraint, which
  would change cuts of trays that work today.

## B5. Fillers perch on trays instead of reaching the floor

An in-level filler runs from **its own level's floor** to the top of the stack, so
a filler beside an upper tray stands on the tray below rather than on the box
floor. Observed in a real project (Star Realms, geometry 0.7.1): one filler sits
on the Counter tray, another on the Deck 2 tray.

Consequences: the filler must be lifted out before the tray under it, which is
the opposite of what a filler should cost; and two fillers at different levels
and different sides cannot merge, so the part count stays higher than it needs
to be.

**Wanted:** fillers reaching the box floor and standing against a wall wherever
possible — one continuous column rather than a perched block.

**Why it is not just a post-pass.** A filler can only reach the floor if every
tray below it is also narrow enough to leave that strip clear. In the observed
stack the bottom tray is full width while the two above are 4.5 mm narrower, so
the slack genuinely only exists from z=8 up. Reaching the floor requires the
packer to prefer **aligning each level's slack to the same side**, so the gaps
line up into one channel. That is a layout-time preference, in the same family
as the filler-count term, and it must be measured the same way — §6 of the
minimal-trays spec records that the related perimeter preference was tried and
dropped because it fragmented fillers rather than pooling them.

Note the current rule is not arbitrary: making fillers only as tall as their own
level left air above them and dropped fill to 69 % on some seeds (see §4.1).
Any change here must keep hard rule 2.

**Measured, and not done (2026-10-16, geometry 0.7.2).** Neither proposed change
works, and the nearest one that does is not worth its cost.

- _Prefer the widest tray at the top_ cannot be built. An upper tray must stand
  inside the tray below it, so the stack footprint is the widest tray's and that
  tray is the bottom one. A level-k filler lies inside the bottom tray's
  footprint by construction, so wherever trays in one stack differ in width it
  stands on the bottom tray, whatever the order.
- _Merge vertically adjacent fillers_ has nothing to join: every in-level filler
  runs to the top of the stack and a level's filler sits beside that level's
  tray, so no filler is ever under another.
- Perching is rare. Over 41 seeds of each of `cards-heavy`, `bits-heavy`,
  `tiles-minis` and `small-tiles` (50 000 iterations) no stored filler perched
  once. Only the Star Realms project does (2 of 6 stored fillers; the Counter
  tray is 91.45 mm wide, the two card trays above it 86.95 mm), and printed it
  is 7 parts and 1 filler either way, because the merge already joins them.
- The one lever that does remove it is a score term per perched filler. At
  20 000 to 30 000 it makes Star Realms re-pack with the Counter turned (no
  perch, 6 stored fillers to 4, fill 99.5 to 99.7 %, link 1 800 to 1 680
  characters) for 41 g more filament (378 to 419 g, +11 %), with the same
  printed parts. At 15 000 and below it does not move that layout. On the four
  fixtures it does nothing (they never perch) beyond search noise, and it never
  buried an _often_ tray (none is buried in any run, with or without it).
  Judged not worth +11 % plastic for a change that does not reduce what is
  printed; reopen if a real project shows a perch that costs a printed part.

- ~~dividers, and how they are held~~ done (geometry 0.7.0, schema 0.1.13, spec §5.2): N dividers
  make N + 1 sections in a grown upright deck or stack of tiles, each held in a slot cut into the
  tray's floor; derived at print time from the stored slots; exclusive with spacer plates. Left
  for later: dividers in bins.
- ~~no spacer the user did not ask for, and the deck stays reachable~~ done (geometry 0.7.2, schema

  loses its 4 default spacers on every seed (15 to 10 printed parts) and its links shrink by about
  100 characters (longest 1969 to 1865). Left: a stored 0.7.1 layout keeps its spacers until its
  plan is run again; dividers have no reachability bound of their own.
